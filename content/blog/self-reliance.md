I have been using Linux on and off for at least the last twelve years. There were times when I tried to go all in, only to come crawling back to a Windows install. By now the pendulum is firmly lodged on the side of Linux; my Windows partition was deleted over two years ago. Since then I was able to learn a lot and help others get started with their journey. With the changing landscape this became easier and easier, but the most important change was in my approach. I'll try and explain this shift here.

There were multiple reasons for the pendulum swings, of course. For one, video games were always a favorite pastime of mine. So when gaming on Linux was made convenient by Steam, moving away from Windows for good became a no-brainer. But why not earlier? Wine, Proton and Bottles were around for a while and you could get most games to run. Still, I never managed to do so. When I tried and eventually failed, I just went back to Windows. This happened again and again. The problem seemed too intimidating and too large of an effort. Realistically speaking, I was right about the effort, but not because of the inherent complexity of the task. I was simply making it harder than it needed to be. 

I got into Linux by way of Arch, I rolled my own desktop environment and customized everything to hell and back. I ripped out everything I didn't think I needed to create the perfect, minimal system. Before you ask, of course I used a tiling window manager. The workflows I set up for myself worked great. I simply could not do anything outside those workflows. Additionally, I did not feel the need to document anything, as the system grew over many years. So when time came to set up gaming and build upon the (functional) mess I created, I was completely overwhelmed. Because I custom-built everything, nothing integrated with existing solutions, that entire teams had poured inordinate amounts of time into. I was also not aware of other ways of doing things, I only knew my own.

Why do this? I got started with Linux during my teenage and early adult years. Back then, I strived for self-reliance above everything. While this comes with the territory of growing up and finding yourself, I believe I practiced this to an unhealthy degree. For example, I never asked anyone for help. Even though the web had communities solely dedicated to helping out with Linux questions, I never reached out. For me the "right" way, was to do it myself, by myself. That way, I ended up reinventing a far amount of wheels.
